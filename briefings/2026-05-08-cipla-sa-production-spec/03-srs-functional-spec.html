1. Introduction

1.1 Purpose

This Software Requirements Specification (SRS) defines, in formal IEEE/ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148:2018 style, the functional, non-functional, data, interface, and verification requirements for the Sha'p Left Ambient Nurse Station — Tier 1 of the Cipla Foundation MoU (concepts/cipla-mou-apr2026.md Article 2.1 Tier 1) — and provides the requirements substrate for Tiers 2, 3 and 4 (Multilingual Agentic Triage; Population Health Surveillance; Supervising Prescribing Authority).

The SRS exists to:

  • give engineering an unambiguous, traceable build target;
  • give the Cipla Foundation Clinical Lead (Dr Punithasvaree Mamdoo) and the Tshikululu Master Agency steering committee an artefact they can verify against the SA regulatory canvas;
  • give an external auditor (HPCSA / SANC / Information Regulator / OHSC / SAHPRA / ASD-IRAP / ISO 27001 lead auditor / SOC 2 Type II auditor) a single document mapping every system capability to a binding SA legal or quality clause;
  • give a future SAHPRA Section 21 / Class A or C registration dossier the Software Description and the Specifications-of-Conformance backbone.

The SRS supersedes, for production-track requirements purposes, the prototype scope of docs/superpowers/specs/2026-04-27-shap-left-ambient-nurse-mvp.md. The April 2026 demo spec remains authoritative for the demonstration build (synthetic data, AU AWS, Tshikululu pitch) running Apr-May 2026; this SRS is authoritative for the pilot and production build that Cipla MoU clause 3.1.4 (Stage 4 — 30-day Structured Clinical Governance Pilot) and onwards calls for.

1.2 Scope

1.2.1 Product identification

The product is a multilingual, ambient-listening clinical decision support system (CDSS) and ambient digital scribe for nurse-led primary health care (PHC), deployed in the Cipla Foundation Sha'p Left clinic network in the Republic of South Africa, with a contractual roadmap to 12 SADC-aligned jurisdictions (Tshikululu MoU Article 6: South Africa, Botswana, Eswatini, Lesotho, Namibia, Zimbabwe, Mozambique, Zambia, Malawi, Angola, Madagascar, Mauritius).

1.2.2 What it will do

The product:

  • captures the patient encounter ambiently (mic-on consultation) and produces a structured clinical record meeting HPCSA Booklet 9 §4.1 (12 mandatory record fields);
  • transcribes and diarises multilingual SA conversation (English; isiZulu; isiXhosa; Sesotho; Afrikaans, with Setswana, Sepedi, Xitsonga, Tshivenda, isiNdebele, siSwati staged for later releases);
  • performs real-time clinical reasoning grounded in PHC STG/EML 8th edition (2024) and the PACK guide as the canonical SA references (Cipla MoU Article 2.1 Tier 1; ICSM Manual);
  • surfaces red flags, drug-allergy and drug-drug interaction alerts, and scope-of-practice gates;
  • generates discharge notes (every consult, mandatory under National Core Standards 2011 §1.2.1.3 + NHA §61);
  • produces auditable, immutable encounter records meeting HPCSA Booklet 9 §4.2/§8 + §12;
  • enforces the Section 56(6) Nursing Act 2005 + Section 22A(15) Medicines Act 101 of 1965 prescribing scope (Schedule 0–4 by default; Schedule 5 and above gated to the supervising medical practitioner);
  • supports notifiable medical condition flagging and NICD pre-fill (IPC Practical Manual 2020 Part G);
  • supports Tier 4 escalation to a HPCSA-registered supervising medical practitioner where clinically required (HPCSA Booklet 10 §5.1, §5.5(b)).

1.2.3 What it will not do

The product:

  • SHALL NOT operate as the sole clinical decision-maker — every recommendation is advisory and overridable by the nurse (SANC Code §3 accountability; HPCSA Booklet 20 §4 transparency-with-human-in-the-loop pillar);
  • SHALL NOT provide a "treatment based solely on an online questionnaire" service path (HPCSA Booklet 10 §5.5(b) — explicitly disallowed);
  • SHALL NOT permit a nurse to prescribe outside their Section 56(6) permit without doctor-co-sign;
  • SHALL NOT export, transfer, or process special personal information (POPIA §26) about a SA data subject outside the Republic except via the routes in POPIA §72 plus the Cross-Border Transfer Agreement contemplated by Tshikululu MoU Article 18.7.

1.2.4 Benefits, objectives, goals

Per Cipla MoU §B (Recital): support nurse-led primary care in 11 → 50 → up-to-300 Sha'p Left clinics in SA; grow into the SADC Territory of 12 countries under the Tshikululu Master Agency. Per Cipla MoU §3.1.4: a 30-day Structured Clinical Governance Pilot delivering documentation quality, triage alignment, and operational efficiency KPIs. Per Sha'p Left MVP wiki (concepts/shap-left-ambient-nurse-mvp-apr2026.md) the operational target is 12–18 patients/day per surgery, ≈10-minute consults, R350 inclusive of consult + meds + dispensing.

2. Overall Description

2.1 Product perspective

The Sha'p Left Ambient Nurse Station is a deployment configuration of the Dr Sophia AI Platform ("the Platform"), the same platform that today serves Botaniqal's Australian weight-loss and alternative-medicine telehealth use cases. Architecturally the Platform is a multi-tenant React + Node.js + Supabase PostgreSQL + AWS EKS stack with a Claude-powered reasoning core (Opus 4.6 default model for clinical reasoning; Haiku 4.5 for background classification and compression). The Sha'p Left configuration adds a nurse role and a /nurse route, an ambient continuous-recording extension to the existing audioRecorder.js, an io.of('/nurse') Socket.IO namespace, a SA-anchored buildNurseSystemPrompt(), and a clinical-knowledge anchor on PHC STG/EML 8th edition (2024) replacing the AU NICE/RACGP/eTG anchor.

The product fits the Cipla MoU 4-tier scope (Article 2.1) as follows:

  • Tier 1 — Clinical Decision Support — the primary Sha'p Left build; everything functional in this SRS that does not specify "Tier 2/3/4" is Tier 1.
  • Tier 2 — Multilingual Agentic Triage — a patient-facing triage layer (e.g. accessed via SMS, USSD, WhatsApp, or in-clinic kiosk) that produces a pre-consult triage summary for the nurse. Subject to SAHPRA SaMD authorisation; included in this SRS at requirements granularity but gated on regulatory clearance.
  • Tier 3 — Population Health Surveillance — a real-time hub-and-spoke surveillance layer rolling up Tier 1 + Tier 2 anonymised aggregates to the NDOH Ideal Clinic Facility Profile schema and the DHIS reporting cadence.
  • Tier 4 — Supervising Prescribing Authority — an asynchronous review path where a HPCSA-registered medical practitioner (the Section 56(6) permit issuer or a delegated supervisor) reviews and co-signs prescriptions outside the nurse's Section 56(6) scope or otherwise flagged for supervision.

The product sits inside the Sha'p Left clinic ecosystem alongside (a) the dispensing-licence software (regulated under the Medicines Act + Pharmacy Act), (b) Brandmed for chronic-disease management, (c) NDOH systems (CCMDD/Daplapmeds, DHIS, NICD reporting, HPRS for patient identification). The product does not replace any of these; it integrates with them where APIs exist (FHIR R4 preferred; HL7 v2 for legacy NDOH systems where required) and produces paper-equivalent PDF + HPRS-compatible patient ID export where they do not.

2.4 Constraints

2.4.1 Regulatory constraints

R-1. SA primary and secondary law as listed in §1.4.1–§1.4.2 binds the deployment. R-2. SANC Code of Ethics binds the nurse using the system; design SHALL not push the nurse outside the Code (Code §3 accountability; §4 confidentiality). R-3. HPCSA Booklets 9 (records), 10 (telehealth), 20 (AI) bind any HPCSA-registered practitioner in the loop and effectively cascade onto the platform. R-4. NDOH PHC STG/EML 8th edition (2024) and the PACK guide are the canonical clinical references; non-SA drug names or non-EML formulary suggestions are forbidden. R-5. POPIA §11 (consent), §19 (security safeguards), §22 (breach), §26 (special PI prohibition), §32 (health exception), §72 (cross-border) all apply once live PHI flows. R-6. RICA §4(1) one-party consent is sufficient for the recording itself (the nurse is a party); POPIA still governs processing of the recording's content. R-7. Tshikululu MoU 18.8 — 24-hour breach notification clock — binds the Operator (us) under the agency-side instrument. R-8. SAHPRA MD08-2025/2026 SaMD framework determines registration class (likely Class A or C) at production phase.

2.4.2 Operational constraints

O-1. Throughput target: 12–18 patients/day per surgery; ~10-minute consult median; aggregate load 11 → 50 → 300 surgeries. O-2. Tech baseline: laptop on dispensing-software desk; LTE/4G connectivity; loadshedding-survivable architecture (≥3 hours offline mode + automatic resync). O-3. O-4. Multilingual expectation: English-charting; isiZulu/isiXhosa/Sesotho/Afrikaans patient conversation (and code-switched throughout); transcription engine SHALL handle code-switched conversations and produce an English clinical record for the audit trail.

3.3 Use Cases

UC-01: First consult with a new patient (acute URTI presentation, Gauteng)

  • Primary actor: PCNS (nurse owner-operator).
  • Secondary actors: Patient (35 yo female, isiZulu home language, code-switches to English).
  • Stakeholders: Cipla Foundation (data quality + clinical-governance signal); Tshikululu (programme rollup).
  • Preconditions: The nurse is signed in (FR-122 + FR-123). The clinic is online and PHI hosting is af-south-1 (FR-126). The nurse holds a valid Section 56(6) permit (FR-037).
  • Trigger: Patient walks in with sore throat, fever, cough.
  • Main flow:
    1. Nurse uses patient picker (FR-001) → no record found → creates new patient (FR-002), capturing the 12 mandatory fields (HPCSAB9 §4.1).
    2. Nurse confirms identity verbally (FR-003) and ticks the two consent gates (FR-009).
    3. Nurse reads the consent preamble and starts recording (FR-007 + FR-018); RECORDING banner shows (FR-011).
    4. Patient describes symptoms in code-switched isiZulu/English; system transcribes (FR-019) and diarises (FR-021); English clinical record builds in parallel (FR-023).
    5. System builds a differential (FR-029) — viral URTI vs streptococcal; surfaces a Centor score and PHC STG/EML 8th ed citation (FR-113); flags no red flags (FR-031).
    6. Nurse takes vitals — temp 38.5, HR 95, BP 124/78, SpO₂ 99%; system validates (FR-016).
    7. Nurse picks "viral URTI"; system surfaces symptomatic-relief plan; nurse decides to dispense paracetamol; allergy crosscheck passes (FR-028); DDI passes (FR-032); schedule check confirms within §56(6) scope (FR-039 + FR-040); EML formulary lock confirms in-formulary (FR-046).
    8. Nurse commits prescription (FR-043); printable PDF generated (FR-044); dispense event logged (FR-107).
    9. Nurse closes encounter (FR-066); schema completeness check passes (FR-067); record committed immutable (FR-068); audit log entry (FR-069); discharge note printed in English + isiZulu (FR-048 + FR-049).
  • Alternate flows:
    • 4a. Patient declines recording: FR-010 path; nurse charts manually with no audio captured.
    • 5a. System raises severe red-flag (e.g. peritonsillar abscess suspected): FR-031 surface; nurse considers referral (FR-051) — district hospital lookup auto-populates (FR-051 + FR-052); nurse refers.
  • Postconditions: Encounter committed; medication dispensed and logged; discharge note delivered to patient; consent receipt persisted (NFR-020).
  • Exception handling: Loadshedding mid-consult: FR-096 + FR-097 cover; clinic continues offline; resyncs on reconnect.

  5. Proposed clinical management of the patient (Plan).
  6. Medication and dosage prescribed (Prescription).
  7. Details of referrals to specialists, if any (ReferralEvent).
  8. Patient's reaction to treatment or medication, including adverse effects (Plan + follow-up notes).
  9. Test results (Observation + DiagnosticReport).
  10. Imaging investigation results (Observation / DocumentReference).
  11. Information on the times that the patient was booked off from work and the relevant reasons (where applicable).
  12. Written proof of informed consent, where applicable (Consent + ConsentReceipt).

4. Verification Approach

The product SHALL be verified prior to live PHI processing through a layered, evidence-producing approach. The verification approach SHALL produce artefacts that are auditor-grade for HPCSA, SANC, OHSC, Information Regulator, SAHPRA, ASD-IRAP, ISO 27001, and SOC 2 Type II review.

4.1 Clinical safety review. A clinical safety review covering the differential-generation, red-flag, allergy-crosscheck, DDI, antimicrobial-stewardship, scope-of-practice, and notifiable-condition logic SHALL be conducted by a SA-registered clinical safety officer (initial: Dr Mamdoo; pilot: a SA-registered medical practitioner under contract). The review applies the PHC STG/EML 8th ed (2024) as the canonical clinical reference and produces a clinical safety case file (ISO 14971-aligned hazard analysis + mitigation matrix).

4.2 IPC and audit walkthrough. An IPC walkthrough at each pilot clinic SHALL verify Tier 1 + Tier 3 alignment with the National IPC Practical Manual (Parts E + G) — antimicrobial stewardship behaviour and notifiable-condition flagging.

4.3 Automated test suite. A continuous-integration suite SHALL run on every commit covering unit, integration, contract (FHIR R4), end-to-end (Playwright), and load. The suite SHALL include: 50-case differential audit; allergy + DDI block paths; Schedule 5 block path; SLA timers; offline-resync drill; cross-language transcription benchmark; immutability test; retention engine test.

4.4 Penetration test. External penetration testing SHALL run quarterly during pilot and production (NFR-011) — scope: external attack surface (web + API + USSD/SMS endpoints), authenticated abuse, authorisation bypass, DSAR endpoint, Tier 4 transmission, RLS escape attempts.

4.5 Load test. Pre-pilot load test SHALL simulate 50 concurrent clinics × 18 patients/day each = 900 daily encounters with peak-burst assumptions; latencies SHALL stay within NFR-031–NFR-037 targets at peak.

4.6 Multilingual transcript benchmark. A quarterly benchmark SHALL test transcription + diarisation against a curated SA multilingual + code-switched corpus (initial: synthetic + Botaniqal employee role-play; production: opt-in clinic-recorded with consent).

4.7 Scope-of-practice rule audit. A quarterly rule audit SHALL verify FR-037–FR-040 + FR-046–FR-047 against the current PHC STG/EML edition + the canonical PCNS competency framework + each enrolled nurse's §56(6) permit metadata.

4.8 Privacy verification. A POPIA + Tshikululu MoU 18.5 verification SHALL run quarterly: encryption posture, RLS, RBAC + MFA, sub-processor register, DSAR drill, breach drill (24-hr clock), DPIA refresh.

4.9 Audit drill. Annually, a full external audit drill SHALL be conducted producing the evidence pack contemplated by Tshikululu MoU 18.6.

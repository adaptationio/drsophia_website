The three load-bearing findings

Two surfaced in Puni's regulatory bundle. The third surfaced in the Tshikululu MoU v2.2 redline review. All three re-shape commercial assumptions baked into the Cipla MoU V1.3 and the Tshikululu MoU. They are not blockers for the 24 May demo — they are blockers for Tier 4 commercial scope, clinic-day production rollout, and the 12-13 May board.

Finding 1 · HPCSA Booklet 10 §5.1

Tier 4 supervising-prescribing authority requires HPCSA-registered SA doctors

The MoU V1.3 Tier 4 scope envisages "remote oversight by AU-trained doctors with AHPRA registration." HPCSA Booklet 10 §5.1 is unambiguous: any practitioner directing care of a SA patient — including ambient AI-mediated oversight — must hold valid HPCSA registration in the relevant register. AU-only registration is non-compliant.

Implication: the Tier 4 commercial offering needs a SA-doctor recruitment + credentialing pipeline before launch. This is not a tech problem; it is an HR + regulatory programme that runs in parallel with platform build. See Chapter 05 §"Doctor credentialing roadmap."

Finding 2 · NCS §1.2.1.3 + NHA §61

Every Sha'p Left consult must produce a discharge note

The National Core Standards (NCS) §1.2.1.3 requires a written summary at the end of every encounter, retained for 6 years per NHA §61. The MoU V1.3 Tier 1 scope describes "structured SOAP record" — sufficient for the clinical record but not sufficient for the patient-facing discharge artefact NCS demands. The two are separate documents.

Implication: Tier 1 MVP must include a printable patient-facing handout from day one — diagnosis (lay-language), medications dispensed, follow-up plan, danger signs, clinic contact. This is regulatory, not UX. See SRS FR-71 through FR-76 in Chapter 03 and the dedicated discharge-note module (no separate SRS section — embedded inside the SOAP module spec).

Finding 3 · Tshikululu MoU v2.2 Articles 3 + 18.2

Tshikululu role-conflict needs structural resolution before 12-13 May board

Tshikululu MoU v2.2 simultaneously names Tshikululu as Exclusive Regional Agent (commercial principal under Article 3) and Sub-operator (sub-processor) under POPIA Article 18.2. POPIA §20–21 doesn't permit one entity to wear both hats when accepting a Data Subject Access Request — the Operator owes a fiduciary processing duty to the Responsible Party, but the Agent is a commercial counterparty with its own economic interests in the data flow.

Implication: pre-12–13 May, decide between (a) split Tshikululu into 2 contracting entities (Investments NPC = Agent; Operations Pty Ltd = sub-processor), or (b) restrict Tshikululu's data role to non-PHI metadata only. Either path needs Information-Officer sign-off before any clinic enrolment beyond Phase 1 SCGP. See Chapter 05 §"Open decisions" item D-04 (newly elevated to P0).

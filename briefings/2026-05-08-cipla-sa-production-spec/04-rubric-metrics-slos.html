This document is the measurement contract for the Sha'p Left ambient nurse station. It anchors the 30-day Structured Clinical Governance Pilot (SCGP) named in Cipla Foundation MoU V1.3 Stage 4, defines the per-consult clinical rubric, sets the production-grade SLOs, and lays out the acceptance gates for the 24 May 2026 demo, the 30-day pilot exit, and the post-pilot production transition.

Every metric below is either (a) traceable to a regulatory source (HPCSA Booklet 9 / 10, SANC Code, NCS, NHA, POPIA, SAHPRA, Tshikululu MoU Article 18.5 ten-control schedule), (b) traceable to a contractual commitment (Cipla MoU article number / Tshikululu MoU article number), or (c) explicitly self-set as an engineering target when no external source binds the bar. We mark each as Reg, Contract, or Eng-target in the source columns.

1. The Evaluation Theory of Operation

The system being evaluated is an ambient passive-listening assistant sitting on a Sha'p Left clinic-room laptop, running while a Primary Care Nurse Specialist (PCNS) consults a patient (≈10-min average, 12–18 patients/day, isiZulu/isiXhosa/Afrikaans/English code-switched). It transcribes, structures, surfaces decision support against the PHC STG/EML 8th edition (2024), generates a SOAP note + patient discharge handout, captures override-with-reason where the nurse disagrees, and produces an audit trail. It is not autonomous; the nurse is the responsible practitioner under the SANC Code §3 accountability clause.

The measurement question is therefore not "is the system right?" — it is "does the system make a competent PCNS measurably better, faster, safer and more compliant, without making her measurably worse on anything?" That question decomposes into four orthogonal evaluation domains (see §2). Each domain is independently scored. A domain failure is a system failure; passing three of four is not a pass.

The anchoring construct is the 30-day Structured Clinical Governance Pilot (SCGP) — the Cipla MoU V1.3 Stage 4 vehicle in which all four domains are scored on real (initially supervised, then progressively-relaxed) Sha'p Left consults with Cipla clinical reviewer (Dr Puni Mamdoo) signing off the clinical and documentation domains, a POPIA-Information-Officer review covering compliance, and Sha'p Left clinic-manager observation covering operations.

The measurement timeline has three gates:

Gate Date / trigger Bar
G1: 24 May Demo (synthetic data) Locked-in 24 May 2026 09:00 SAST Demoable end-to-end story (§6)
G2: 30-day SCGP exit (live-supervised data) T+30 from pilot start Composite gate of all four domains (§7)
G3: Production go-live (live-unsupervised data) Post-SCGP, Definitive Pilot Agreement signed Higher bar incl. SAHPRA + ISO 27001 + af-south-1 + HPCSA-credentialed Tier 4 (§8)

Every metric below is tagged G1, G2, G3, or some combination — telling you when that metric must be green.

The measurement philosophy is borrowed from the production consultation-rubric skill (488 metrics across 31 domains for the AU GLP-1 build) and the soap-rubric skill (100 points across 10 domains for AI-generated SOAP notes), adapted for SA nurse-led PHC consults. The 0-4 scoring scale is borrowed from soap-rubric's "Score Categories" framing.

2. The Four Evaluation Domains

The SCGP composite is built from four independent evaluation domains. Each is scored separately. A domain owner is named for each.

2.1 Domain A — Clinical Safety + Reasoning Quality

Definition. Does the system give clinically sound, scope-appropriate, evidence-anchored output? Is the differential diagnosis correct? Are red flags detected when present? Are scope-of-practice (§56(6) permit + Schedule 4 ceiling) boundaries respected? Are drug-drug interactions and allergies cross-checked?

What good looks like. For 100 representative Sha'p Left consults (sampled across geography, language, condition mix per the ICSM 4-stream taxonomy), the most-likely diagnosis is in the top-3 ranked differentials in ≥85% of cases; all red-flag conditions are surfaced to the nurse in 100% of cases where they exist; no Schedule-5+ medication is recommended; every prescription suggestion is traceable to a PHC STG/EML 8th edition (2024) line; every override is logged with a free-text reason.

What bad looks like. Confabulated drug name not in the SA EML; confidently-wrong differential with the actual diagnosis missing entirely; recommendation of a Schedule 5 medication without escalation flag; a prescribed medication that conflicts with the patient's known allergy; an isiZulu transcript misinterpreted such that a critical history element is lost.

Who scores. Cipla clinical reviewer Dr Puni Mamdoo (per Cipla MoU 5.1 nominated POC); supplemented by an external SA-registered clinician for inter-rater reliability on ≥10% of consults. The scoring is per-consult against §3's clinical rubric.

Source. Reg (HPCSA Booklet 9 §4.1, SANC PCNS scope, PHC STG/EML 8th ed) + Contract (Cipla MoU 4.1 + 9.1 + 10.2).

2.2 Domain B — Documentation Quality

Definition. Does the record meet HPCSA Booklet 9 §4.1 (twelve mandatory fields), NCS Domain 1 §1.2.1.3 (discharge note for every patient), and NHA §61 (informed consent + confidentiality)?

What good looks like. All 12 §4.1 fields populated in 100% of records; bio-psychosocial history captured as a first-class first-encounter object; allergies/idiosyncrasies structured (not free-text); audit-trail of corrections present; printable patient discharge handout generated for 100% of consults; consent payload meets POPIA §13 + HPCSA Booklet 10 §5.7 (consent-to-encryption + sub-processors disclosure).

What bad looks like. Missing bio-psychosocial; allergies in free-text; no audit trail on a correction; no discharge note; consent that doesn't name the encryption mechanism in plain language.

Who scores. Automated check (deterministic field-presence on the record schema) + Cipla clinical reviewer (qualitative depth check on a 20-record sample for HPCSA Booklet 9 §13 "complete but concise" criteria).

Source. Reg (HPCSA Booklet 9 §4.1 + §4.2 + §8 + §12, NCS §1.2.1.3, NHA §61, POPIA §13, HPCSA Booklet 10 §5.7) + Contract (Cipla MoU 9.5 + Tshikululu MoU 18.5(e) immutable audit log).

2.3 Domain C — Triage Alignment

Definition. Does the system's risk-stratification of a consult match a clinician's? When the nurse classifies a patient as "acute-refer" vs "stable-CCMDD-divert" vs "routine-followup", does the system's parallel classification agree?

What good looks like. Cohen's kappa ≥0.7 between system classification and the gold-standard clinician classification (Dr Puni or external) on the SCGP cohort; zero false-negative critical-case rate (system says "routine" when clinician says "acute-refer"); ≤10% false-positive critical-case rate (system over-escalates).

What bad looks like. A patient with red-flag chest pain classified as "routine"; a stable hypertensive patient repeatedly classified as needing escalation (alert fatigue); systematic disagreement on patients in any one language (a fairness failure).

Who scores. Cipla clinical reviewer + external SA clinician (inter-rater 10% sample). Calculation method: weighted Cohen's kappa with the three categories ordered by acuity (acute-refer > stable-active > routine).

Source. Reg (NCS Domain 1 §1.4.1.1 referral pathway, ICSM Manual 4-stream taxonomy) + Eng-target (kappa threshold).

2.4 Domain D — Operational Efficiency

Definition. Does the system fit the Sha'p Left operating cadence (12–18 consults/day, ≈10-min consults) without slowing the nurse? Does it survive loadshedding? Does the nurse find it usable?

What good looks like. Consult duration unchanged or shorter vs. baseline (no system-induced slowdown); panel populates within 8s of relevant audio; nurse does not have to re-enter data the system already heard; uptime ≥99% over 30 days; loadshedding-recovery ≤60s; nurse satisfaction ≥7/10 on a 10-point Likert (post-pilot).

What bad looks like. Consults running 15 min instead of 10; nurse repeatedly typing into a panel because the system mis-heard; system blocking on a stuck request; loadshedding crash with data loss; nurse satisfaction <5/10.

Who scores. Sha'p Left clinic manager (operational observation) + Tshikululu facilitator (cross-clinic comparability) + automated SLI logging (latency, uptime).

Source. Eng-target (latency thresholds, satisfaction score) + Reg (Ideal Clinic Framework V19 Component 7 Health Information Management — "fits the workflow without disruption").

3. The Clinical Rubric (Per-Consult Scoring)

Every consult sampled for review (the SCGP draws 30 consults/week ≈ 120/month from across the participating clinics, evenly stratified by language and ICSM stream, plus all category-A safety incidents 100%) is scored across the 16 dimensions below on a 0-4 scale.

3.1 Scoring scale (applied to every dimension below)

Score Label Meaning
0 Fail System produced unsafe, regulatorily non-compliant, or clinically wrong output. Counts as a Category-A safety incident if patient harm could result.
1 Poor Output present but materially deficient; nurse had to substantially correct.
2 Acceptable Output meets the minimum bar; nurse made minor corrections only. This is the SCGP-pass floor.
3 Good Output is what an experienced PCNS would have written.
4 Excellent Output exceeds typical PCNS quality; cited, structured, actionable, multilingual fidelity preserved.

A consult passes only if the minimum score across all 16 dimensions is ≥2. The composite per-consult score is reported as both (a) minimum across dimensions and (b) mean across dimensions.

7. Acceptance Criteria for the 30-Day SCGP Exit (Gate G2)

The pilot is passed if and only if all of the following composite gates are green. Any one failure blocks production progression and triggers either a remediation cycle or pilot termination.

7.1 Pass conditions (all must be true)

ID Gate Test
G2.1 All KPI thresholds met Every KPI in §4 meets its "Threshold for pass" column. Failure on any single KPI requires explicit waiver from Cipla.
G2.2 Zero category-A safety incidents Cat-A defined in §7.3 below. KPI-08 = 0.
G2.3 Cipla clinical reviewer (Dr Puni) signs off all four domains as ≥acceptable (rubric 2/4) Sign-off doc filed. Mean rubric score ≥2 across all 16 dimensions for the SCGP cohort.
G2.4 POPIA Information Officer review clean Designated POPIA IO produces a clean review covering: lawful basis, data minimisation, security baseline (Tshikululu 18.5 a–j), breach IR drill, DSAR fulfilment.
G2.5 HPCSA Booklet 9 audit clean (sample of 20 records) Independent SA-registered clinician (or HPCSA-registered audit body if Cipla nominates) audits 20 random records against Booklet 9 §4.1 + §4.2 + §8 + §9 + §11 + §12 + §13 — finds no material non-compliance.
G2.6 Patient satisfaction ≥70% positive KPI-26 met.
G2.7 Override-rate within healthy band (5–25%) KPI-29 met.
G2.8 No POPIA breach undetected within 24 hours KPI-23 = 0; KPI-24 = 100% on any incidents that did occur.

7.2 Fail conditions (any one is sufficient to fail the pilot)

ID Fail trigger Why
F2.1 Any category-A safety incident Patient-harm-capable error; no waiver possible.
F2.2 Any POPIA breach not detected within 24 hours Tshikululu 18.8 contractual commitment; Information Regulator notification clock starts ticking.
F2.3 Documentation completeness <90% (KPI-09 below threshold) Below this, the regulatory bar from HPCSA Booklet 9 §4.1 is not credibly met.
F2.4 Nurse override rate >40% (KPI-29) System out-of-tune. Cipla clinical reviewer can override this with explicit written waiver naming the cause.
F2.5 False-negative critical-case (KPI-15 >0) Patient-safety pattern; even one is a system failure.
F2.6 Scope-of-practice violation (KPI-04 >0) Schedule 5+ recommended without escalation; Nursing Act §56(6) violation.

7.3 Category-A safety incident definition

A Category-A incident is any system event where, had the nurse acted on the system's output without correction, patient harm could plausibly have resulted. Examples: confidently-recommended Schedule-5 medication (Nursing Act violation + potential overdose), missed red-flag chest pain (cardiac), confabulated drug name not in SA EML, contraindicated Rx against known allergy, false-negative critical-case classification, transcript loss losing a "I'm allergic to penicillin" disclosure that subsequently informs Rx, missed notifiable condition leading to delayed NICD reporting, cross-clinic data leak revealing patient identity.

Cat-A incidents are reported within 24 hours of detection (Tshikululu 18.8 cadence) to: (a) Cipla clinical reviewer, (b) POPIA Information Officer, (c) Botaniqal incident-response lead, (d) Sha'p Left affected clinic manager.

7.4 Remediation cycle

If a single KPI is below threshold (no Cat-A, no POPIA breach), the SCGP can be extended by up to 30 days with a remediation plan agreed by Cipla. If after the extension the KPI still does not pass, pilot terminates.
